# Using words from the source video

Owner rule, 2026-11-12: "we can use some words from the video to make our video better."

Before this rule, scripts had to paraphrase everything from the source. Now a script may borrow **some of the source's own wording** when it says the idea better than a paraphrase would.

## What is allowed

- **Short, vivid phrases:** a striking term, a metaphor, a memorable way of putting something. Keep each borrowed phrase short (about a dozen words or fewer).
- **Plain everyday wording** the speaker used for a feeling or a situation, when it is the way a viewer would say it too.
- **A handful per video.** The script is still our own writing; borrowed phrases are seasoning.

## What stays the same

- **No names.** Do not name or credit the podcast, the host or the guest, on screen, in narration or in the description. This rule did not change.
- **No long passages.** Do not lift sentences in a row, a whole argument, or a story told the way the speaker told it. Rebuild the structure ourselves.
- **Do not present borrowed words as a quotation.** No quotation marks around them, no "as one expert said".
- **Facts still need a primary source.** A borrowed phrase that carries a factual claim (a number, a study result, a cause) is only usable once that claim is verified and in the fact ledger. If the source's wording overstates the evidence, use our own accurate wording.
- **Viewer-first rule, 8-minute rule, narrator, subtitles and QC** are unaffected.

## How to work

1. While reading the transcript, collect candidate phrases in `studio-output/<slug>/research/source-phrases.md` with their transcript line or timestamp.
2. In the script draft, mark each one used.
3. The reality checker confirms each borrowed phrase is short, unnamed, not in quotation marks, and makes no unverified claim.

## Why the limits

Short phrases keep the script original and keep the channel clear of copying someone else's video. If the owner wants to go further (longer excerpts, or naming the source), that is a separate decision to state explicitly.
