## Directly supported production lessons

In the behind-the-scenes account, Alan describes deciding the story and ending, using timed dummy characters for a handoff, and replacing those with refined character animation. He reports slowing a sequence after realizing its distinct events were hard to process. These are concrete revision decisions, not proof of a universal cadence or guaranteed retention. [Published captions](https://filmot.com/sidebyside/F74m1Bi9Trc/en/auto.en/English/English%2B%28auto-generated%29/Behind%2Bthe%2BScenes%2B-%2BAnimation%2Bvs.%2BYouTube/Alan%2BBecker).

That account says storyboards were unnecessary for that particular flat interface film. Do not turn this into a general no-storyboard rule: his 2018 Nether post explicitly describes making an animatic before team production. The useful takeaway is a clear timed handoff appropriate to the scene. [Creator post](https://www.patreon.com/alanbecker/posts/nether-avm-8-19537806).

[Official credits](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2I9l4a_7kw).

His earlier AMA distinguishes making a drawing attractive from making it move convincingly. ## Principles to turn into original practice

His tutorial covers the established twelve principles; he credits Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston. Brief source summary: preparation makes actions legible; staging directs attention; key poses structure movement; overlapping motion, arcs, spacing and timing shape performance; exaggeration and appealing design support clarity. Read the actual tutorial rather than interpreting these labels as an automatic recipe. [Captions](https://filmot.com/sidebyside/uDqjIdI4bF4/en/en/English/English/12%2BPrinciples%2Bof%2BAnimation%2B%28Official%2BFull%2BSeries%29).

The following exercise design and acceptance checks are **our proposals**, not observations or measured settings from Alan's films.

| Practice area | FounderNotes exercise | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Intent and silhouette | Draw the Youth uncertain, frustrated, curious and relieved without text or facial detail | Can a reviewer distinguish the intentions? |
| Preparation | Before pulling the gate, plant the feet, move the torso and establish the grip | Does the effort have a visible source? |
| Contact and force | Keep the grip on the bar during effort; release it before recovering | Do the hand, feet and body obey the same contact state? |
| Pose sequence | Block reach, grip, effort, failure and reassessment before adding in-betweens | Is each key pose different and necessary? |
| Timing contrast | Give the Youth an impulsive attempt; give the Philosopher a considered response | Are the characters distinct through behavior? |
| Gaze and attention | Youth notices a precise map feature before turning toward the route | Does the audience know what changed? |
| Curved motion | Explore an original curved reach while preserving contact at its endpoint | Does the limb read as articulated rather than sliding? |
| Body mechanics | Lift one stair foot while the other bears weight | Does the torso's movement agree with its support? |
| Settling | After a stop, let the torso/head settle in an intentional order | Is it a reaction rather than perpetual idle motion? |
| Clear staging | Remove background details competing with the map interaction | Is the important action readable on a phone? |
| Secondary action | Add a small hand hesitation to a larger reach only if it clarifies uncertainty | Does it support the primary action? |
| Sound | Once narration exists, place a small contact cue at the important physical event | Does listening confirm sync and comfortable balance? |

Avoid prescribing one easing curve for all movement. Effort, hesitation, recognition and walking need different timing decisions. The export frame rate is not a performance score; more frames cannot fix an unclear pose or a contradictory action.

## Original stick-character direction

Start with a small original cast. The Youth's baseline can be forward-weighted and restless; the Philosopher's can be settled and economical. Treat these as testable acting choices, not a repetitive gesture assigned to every line. Differentiate them through proportions, posture, timing and original costume cues only when needed. Check readability without relying on color alone.

Create a pose sheet, proportions guide, contact states and a small set of settings. Use a restrained original palette and quiet textured backgrounds where helpful. A useful foreground prop should have a physical location, owner, hand contact and continuity through the cut. Diagrams must remain accurate and captions readable.

Do not transplant Alan's named characters, signature cast, scene choreography, interfaces, music or source files. His storefront expressly restricts commercial gain from its downloads; no source purchase or reuse was made. [Creator storefront](https://alanbecker.gumroad.com/).

## Application to our latest study

The existing 24-second silent Cut 3a is a blocking reference for our own defects, not an Alan-style result. It has basic cause/effect, a revised map gesture and stair contacts, but limited poses, repetitive framing, simple environments and no narration or sound. Its three passing tests cover rendering, ink reveal and arm lengths. They cannot establish acting quality or channel parity.

Before adding more scenes, make one original 6-10-second stick-character interaction that reads in silhouette: approach the gate, discover resistance, attempt a different physical strategy, then reassess. This is an exercise brief only; it has not been rendered in this study. The next production cycle should test the craft rather than relabel the current figure style.

After the performance is readable, build the actual 60-90-second narrated pilot on measured audio. Keep viewer-first preparation and full-production review. Our educational format still needs explicit, verified explanations; silent narrative competence alone does not establish that a viewer learned the intended concept.
